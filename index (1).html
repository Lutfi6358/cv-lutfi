Ahmad Lutfi Afifi bin Mohd Nasir

Research Assistant and PhD candidate East Coast Environmental Research Institute, Universiti Sultan Zainal Abidin

Whether the new crescent moon can be seen on a given evening decides when the Islamic month begins. I work on answering that question with instruments and code rather than the naked eye alone, building detection systems that find the hilal in images taken at Balai Cerap KUSZA and other observatories in Malaysia.

About

Falak on one side, computer vision on the other.

My training started in Islamic studies. I took a Diploma and then a Bachelor of Islamic Studies in Syariah at Universiti Sultan Zainal Abidin, specialising in Islamic astronomy, with a thesis on the legal analysis of prayer in high-altitude areas and high-rise buildings. A Master of Science in Astronomy followed, on using image processing and enhancement techniques to detect new crescent moon visibility.

That sequence is the reason I work the way I do. Hilal determination is a question of fiqh before it is a question of optics, and a detection system that ignores either half is not much use to the people who have to make the decision. So the work runs in both directions: applying deep learning and image enhancement to observation data, and keeping the output legible to the criteria that isbat actually uses.

I am based at Balai Cerap KUSZA with the East Coast Environmental Research Institute, where my work covers crescent detection, sky brightness and light pollution monitoring across Malaysian observation sites, and the observation systems themselves. Part of that has meant taking the systems out of the lab, including teaching, installing and running hands-on training on the HOTOH system for the Terengganu State Mufti Office.

Research

Four threads that keep meeting in the same problem.

  • Islamic astronomy

    Hisab and rukyah, the fiqh basis for moon sighting, and how classical falak sources translate into criteria a modern system can be held to.

  • Hilal observation and visibility

    Field observation at Malaysian sites, the limits of optical equipment for a thin crescent, and how sky brightness and light pollution constrain what is recoverable.

  • Image processing

    Contrast enhancement and transform-based methods for pulling a low-contrast crescent out of a bright twilight sky, including Circular Hough Transform detection.

  • Artificial intelligence and computer vision

    Object detection applied to crescent imagery, including YOLO-based approaches, aimed at automated detection that runs on live observation rather than archived data.
