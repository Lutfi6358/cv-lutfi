Hilal detection

Finding the crescent

The core line of work. Each system takes the same problem a step further, from a hybrid observation method, to image processing in software, to an integrated tracker.

Patent filed2024

HORTOH

Hybrid Observation Roslan Technique of Hilal

The observation method the rest of the work is built on. A thin crescent close to the horizon is often below what the eye can separate from the twilight behind it, so HORTOH pairs optical observation with image capture and processing, letting the sighting be recorded and checked rather than only reported. That matters for isbat, where a claim that can be re-examined carries more weight than one that cannot.

The method was filed as a patent in 2024 and first described in the IEICES proceedings paper on the implementation of the technique.

HOTOH 2.0

Hybrid Observation Technique of Hilal 2.0: image processing for hilal visibility detection using Python

The second generation, and the first to exist as software rather than a procedure. HOTOH 2.0 implements the enhancement and detection pipeline in Python, so an observation image can be processed on site and the crescent brought out of a low-contrast twilight sky while the observation window is still open.

It is the system that has travelled furthest outside the university. In September 2026 it was installed at the Terengganu State Mufti Office with hands-on training, so that the office which has to make the determination can run the system itself.

MySIGHT

Malaysia Smart Integrated Global Hilal Tracker

Detection on a single evening is one problem. Knowing what was seen, from where, under what sky, across years of attempts is another. MySIGHT brings hilal observation records and site conditions from Malaysian observation sites into one system, so that an individual sighting can be read against the record rather than in isolation.

It is the most recent of the registered systems and the one closest to the PhD work, since a detector is only as good as the observations it can be tested against.

Sky quality

What the sky allows

A detector cannot recover what the sky has already washed out. This side of the work measures and models the conditions that set the limit.

Copyright2025

SkySight

Spatial model for human perception and astronomical visibility in light pollution studies

Light pollution is usually reported as a brightness figure at a point. SkySight models it as something a person at a site actually experiences, joining sky brightness measurements to a spatial model of what remains visible, which is the form the question takes when an observation site is being chosen or defended.

The measurements behind it come from the light pollution monitoring work at KUSZA Observatory, Langkawi National Observatory and the Terengganu hilal observation sites.

Falak through hybrid methods

Pengajaran dan pembelajaran subjek falak melalui kaedah hibrid: permainan interaktif dan implementasi augmented reality

The AL-KAHF card game and its augmented reality layer, built so that constellations, lunar phases and the geometry behind a sighting can be handled rather than only diagrammed. It has since been used in school STEM carnivals as well as university teaching, and the classroom results were published in Jurnal Fizik Malaysia.

It is the most decorated of the outputs, with four medals across university and international teaching innovation competitions in 2023.

Astrotourism at KUSZA Observatory

Astrotourism: elevating user experience and enriching astronomy knowledge at KUSZA Observatory, Universiti Sultan Zainal Abidin

A visitor programme that treats Balai Cerap KUSZA as a public building as well as a research site. It sets out what a visitor sees, in what order, and what they should be able to explain afterwards, which turns an observatory tour into something with a teaching shape.

It has been recognised in both innovation and tourism competitions, including a Best Design Award at ITIEC 2025.
